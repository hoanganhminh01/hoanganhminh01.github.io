This lecture presented a case study on applying deep learning for fault detection and diagnosis in photovoltaic (PV) modules using electroluminescence (EL) images. Students were introduced to the ELPV dataset, key exploratory data analysis steps, and the challenges of distinguishing functional, marginal, and defective solar cells. The session covered preprocessing, statistical comparison of image features, contrast visualization, and dimensionality reduction using PCA and t-SNE.

I then outlined a “caviar strategy” for model selection, comparing multiple supervised architectures (VGG19, ResNet152v2, InceptionResNetv2, NasNetLarge) and an unsupervised autoencoder. Results showed that VGG19 achieved the best overall performance, while the reconstruction model failed to localize defects effectively. The lecture concluded with a discussion on limitations, overfitting issues, and future directions such as hyperparameter tuning and exploring U-Net-style architectures for more precise fault localization.
